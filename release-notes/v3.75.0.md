# Blender Buddy v3.75.0 — animation's sixty-eighth piece: looking behind the other way

One new guided flow, and nothing else changes. Built and
held locally under the owner's 2026-11-26 release-batching
rule — this is batch bookkeeping, not a separate release;
it ships together with the first batched release. The
public download still serves v3.20.0.

**Make it look behind the other way** sits under "Learn by doing" beside the
modeling, sculpting and first sixty-seven animation
pieces — Buddy's sixty-eighth animation piece, teaching
looking behind the other way, in four steps:

1. **Pick the thing that will look behind the other way** — one thing that will
   turn right around the other way to face behind and then come back to facing forward.
2. **Remember its starting facing** — a facing
   memory at frame 1, level, rotation (0, 0, 0).
3. **Look behind the other way and come back** —
   rotation Z to -3.1416 remembered at frame 24, and
   back to facing forward, rotation (0, 0, 0),
   remembered at frame 48,
   the side axes staying level and the place held the whole way.
4. **Watch it look behind the other way, then save** — played across the
   span, and the file saved with the facing memories
   in it.

The teaching point is that the other way is the opposite half circle:
the look-behind piece turned a thing the whole half circle one way — this piece turns
it the whole half circle the other way, reading facings
(0, 0, 0) / (0, 0, -3.1416) / (0, 0, 0) at frames
1/24/48 in the reference build ((0, 0, -1.5708) partway back at frame 36 as well). The checks sample the real evaluated facing
across the span: the span must run at least 20 frames,
the peak (most negative) turn on the upright axis must land between -3.5 and -2.8, the end turn must settle within 0.35 of the starting facing, the side axes must hold within 0.35, and the
thing must stay within 0.5 of its place. A probe on Blender 5.3.0 and 4.2.5
before building showed the facing memories
level at frame 1, turned -3.1416 on the Z axis at frame 24 and level again at frame 48 read back (span 47, peak
-3.1416, end 0.0, side drift 0.0, place drift 0.0 in the probe); a look behind the first way read peak 0.0,
a turn around the other way read peak -3.1416 with end -3.1416, a spin the other way read peak -6.2832 with end -6.2832, a wag read peak -0.4, a small look the other way read peak -1.0 with end -1.0, a tilt read peak 0.0 with side drift 1.0, and a look behind the other way that stayed
read end -3.1416 — all failing the bar. The probe ran on Blender 5.3.0 and 4.2.5 with byte-identical outputs.

Negative cases are in the battery alongside the walk:
a plain scene ticks only step 1, a starting
facing alone does not prove the look behind the other way, a look behind the first way does not
prove it, a turn around the other way does not prove it, a spin the other way does not prove it, a wag does not prove it, a small look the other way does not prove it, a tilt does not prove it, and a look behind the other way that stays does not prove it either.

Verified on Blender 5.3.0 and 4.2.5 at the final
v3.75.0 source: the flows battery (all seventy-six
flows plus the anim_otherlookback negatives), the interface
battery, and the plain-layer and search batteries all
pass on both versions. Proof picture: demo/anim_otherlookback_final.png — the
reference build's red thing at frame 24, turned the whole half circle the other way
at facing (0, 0, -3.1416) in its own place (a cube reads the same turned around, so the numeric proof carries it, as with the look pieces).
