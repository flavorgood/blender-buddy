# Blender Buddy v3.21.0 — animation's fourteenth piece: flipping

One new guided flow, and nothing else changes.

**Make it flip** sits under "Learn by doing" beside the
modeling, sculpting and first thirteen animation pieces —
Buddy's fourteenth animation piece, teaching flipping, in
four steps:

1. **Pick the thing that will flip** — one thing whose
   facing will go all the way over.
2. **Remember its upright facing** — a facing memory at
   frame 1, level, that the flip will land back on.
3. **Flip it all the way over, and land it** — half a
   turn remembered at frame 24 (upside down), one full
   turn remembered at frame 48 (upright again).
4. **Watch it flip, then save it** — played across the
   span, and the file saved with all three facing
   memories in it.

The teaching point is that a facing journey can go all
the way around: upright, upside down, upright again,
and Blender filling in the roll between them — a
quarter of the way across, at frame 12, it reads
0.9175 rad of the turn. The checks read the facing
memories themselves and the real evaluated facing
sampled across the whole span: it must run at least
20 frames, the total turn must reach at least 5.0 rad,
the turn must really pass upside down (at least 2.8 rad
from the start), and the ends must land on the same
facing, allowing whole turns. A probe on Blender 5.3.0
and 4.2.5 before building showed facing memories at 0,
pi and 2*pi across frames 1/24/48 read back and
interpolate identically on both versions.

Negative cases are in the battery alongside the walk:
a plain scene ticks only step 1, an upright start alone
does not prove the flip, a half flip that ends upside
down does not prove it either, and a rock back and
forth does not prove it.

Verified on Blender 5.3.0 AND 4.2.5 at the final v3.21.0
source: the flows battery (all twenty-two flows plus
the anim_flip negatives), the interface battery, and
the plain-layer and search batteries all pass on both
versions. Proof picture: demo/anim_flip_final.png —
the reference build's red cone at frame 24, upside
down at the middle of its flip, tip on the ground.
